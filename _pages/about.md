Growing up in a rural area with a single disabled parent profoundly influenced my _**research trajectory**_ and multiplied my commitment to advancing systemic change in educational practice for underserved students. In my PhD program in Technology at Purdue  Polytechnic, I focus on improving STEM teaching practices in a technology-rich environment. My research intersects the design of accessible, flexible learning content with learning sciences and the development of advanced learning technologies to improve cognitive and non-cognitive participation of undergraduate students with disabilities in STEM courses in higher education. Using _**AI-driven learning analytics**_ and action-oriented research methods, the theoretical framing draws upon _**emancipatory approaches**_, learning theories, and Self-Determination Theory that support basic psychological needs and foster engagement. The goal is to develop evidence-based, scalable instructional models and digitally accessible materials that empower students to learn and faculty to teach STEM in an accessible, technology-rich environment, thereby increasing student success.

I work with <a href="https://polytechnic.purdue.edu/profile/nmentzer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Dr. Nathan Mentzer</a> as a Graduate Research Assistant on the National Science Foundation - funded Project "Expanding Accessibility of Learning through Blended Synchronous Instruction of Face-to-face and Remote Students,” award # 2110799. I leverage learning analytics, advanced statistical analysis, and machine learning (e.g., t-test, regression, path analysis, equivalence test, NLP, topic modelling) to inform instructional design practices in technology-enhanced environments. To date, I have presented more than 10 peer-reviewed conference papers and published in leading journals and venues, including the _Journal of Research on Technology Education_ and _Educational Technology Research and Development_.

As a former special educator, consultant to UNICEF Azerbaijan on inclusive education, and trainer in multiple disability-focused professional development projects, I am committed to establishing a proactive approach to improving the accessibility of STEM education through research and advocacy for students with disabilities. I have been recognized for my contributions through highly competitive, merit-based scholarships and fellowships, multiple travel awards, and induction into the Honor Society of Phi Kappa Phi (ФКФ), where I served as Student Vice President for two consecutive years. I have been collaborating and mentoring future talents since 2023, aiming to leverage my experience and knowledge to drive meaningful change in the field.

I hold a B.S. in Special Education and an M.S. in Teaching and Curriculum with a concentration in instructional design.
