I am a Ph.D. candidate in Technology at Purdue  Polytechnic. My research intersects the design of digitally accessible, flexible learning content with the development of advanced learning technologies to improve cognitive and non-cognitive participation of undergraduate students with disabilities in STEM courses in higher education. Using AI-driven learning analytics and action-oriented research methods, the theoretical framing draws upon emancipatory approaches, learning theories, and Self-Determination Theory that support basic psychological needs and foster engagement. The goal is to develop evidence-based, scalable instructional models and digitally accessible materials that empower students to learn and faculty to teach STEM in an accessible, technology-rich environment, thereby increasing student success.

I worked with <a href="https://polytechnic.purdue.edu/profile/nmentzer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Dr. Nathan Mentzer</a> as a Graduate Research Assistant on the National Science Foundation - funded Project "Expanding Accessibility of Learning through Blended Synchronous Instruction of Face-to-face and Remote Students,” award # 2110799. I leveraged learning analytics, advanced statistical analysis, and machine learning (e.g., t-test, regression, path analysis, equivalence test, NLP, topic modelling) to inform curriculum development and lesson planning, particularly in environments where educational technologies are heavily utilised. To date, I have presented more than 10 peer-reviewed conference papers and published in leading journals and venues, including the _Journal of Research on Technology Education_, _Educational Technology Research and Development_, and _Education Sciences_.

Growing up in a rural area with a single disabled parent profoundly influenced my _**research trajectory**_ and multiplied my commitment to advancing systemic change in educational practice for underserved students. I argue that although educators in higher education recognize the needs of underserved students, including those with disabilities, most STEM practitioners overlook or simply prefer not to discuss the need to accommodate those students' needs during course design and instruction. 

As a former special educator, consultant to UNICEF Azerbaijan on inclusive education, and trainer in multiple disability-focused professional development projects, I am committed to establishing a proactive approach to improving the accessibility of STEM education through research and advocacy for students with disabilities. I have been recognized for my contributions through highly competitive, merit-based scholarships and fellowships, multiple travel awards, and induction into the Honor Society of Phi Kappa Phi (ФКФ), where I served as Student Vice President for two consecutive years. My career goal is to pursue an academic position as an Assistant Professor upon completing my Ph.D. I have been collaborating and mentoring future talents since 2023, aiming to leverage my experience and knowledge to drive meaningful change in the field.

I hold a B.S. in Special Education and an M.S. in Teaching and Curriculum with a concentration in instructional design.
